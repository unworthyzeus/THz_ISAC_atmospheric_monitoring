Calibration and Acquisition Requirements for Sub
THz Pollutant Sensing
Guillem Moreno Garcia
Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya
Email: guillem.moreno.garcia@estudiantat.upc.edu
Abstract—We study conditional pollutant retrieval using lay-
ered HITRAN spectroscopy, real Beijing concentration and
weather records, and simulated radio attenuation. A convex esti-
mator balances atmospheric bias, persistent calibration error and
acquisition noise. We evaluate differential measurements, struc-
tured calibration assumptions and jointly optimized frequencies
and dwell. At 100 seconds of charged sequential acquisition, ﬁne-
grid dwell design reduces worst normalized NO2 error from
3.513 to 2.340 under an arbitrary ±10−4 dB calibration box.
An exact represented-model dual certiﬁcate still gives a noise-free
ﬂoor of 1.923. A stronger polynomial calibration assumption with
10−5 dB arbitrary residual yields error 1.363; a frozen design
reaches the unit target at 248.809 seconds in its declared envelope.
Temporal differences reduce modeled hourly atmospheric bias
but incur reference noise. Eleven of 24 weather pairs leave the
nominal SNR regime. These computations identify conditional
calibration and resource requirements; they do not establish
measured drift or ﬁeld retrieval.
Index Terms—Atmospheric sensing, calibration, generalized
least squares, HITRAN, integrated sensing and communication.
I. QUESTION AND CONTRIBUTION
Shared communication and absorption sensing has a prece-
dent for atmospheric water vapour [1]; multi-medium prop-
agation models motivate treating molecular and particle at-
tenuation jointly [2]. Ambient pollutant retrieval additionally
requires separating a weak target spectrum from background
uncertainty. A small local lower bound alone cannot establish
an achievable measurement error.
We ask which error requirements follow when that miss-
ing estimator is supplied. Our contribution is a conditional
joint atmospheric/calibration estimator, machine-checkable
represented-model bias lower bounds, and three construc-
tive NO2 interventions evaluated with charged sequential
acquisition. New frequencies and jointly optimized dwell
are separated from changes to the calibration assumptions
and from relative-change measurements. Generalized least
squares, Fisher information and convex experimental design
are established methods; this work applies these principles
to the declared spectroscopy and link rather than claiming a
new general estimation principle [14]. Earlier probe-selection,
satellite-column and regression studies are retained in the
appendix as supporting diagnostics.
II. EVIDENCE AND FIXED PHYSICAL MODEL
The primary spectroscopy input is the archived 9,340-line
HITRAN table, checked against HAPI using the same line
records [5], [6]. Real UCI Beijing records provide mea-
sured surface concentrations and weather [8]. They do not
provide vertical proﬁles or synchronized radio attenuation.
The chronological split and concentration-order exclusions are
unchanged. All current atmospheric summaries use training
rows only; both training quantile scenarios and reused test-
period regressions are explicitly separate from independent
ﬁeld validation.
Four reported errors are normalized by ﬁxed concentration
magnitudes: CO 4,000, O3 100, SO2 40 and NO2 25 µg m−3.
These numbers originate in WHO guideline values [12]; they
are magnitude references, not detection probabilities, instanta-
neous compliance thresholds or a common averaging-period
speciﬁcation. Simulated estimates are not treated as health
measurements.
The 24-layer atmosphere extends from 0 to 12 km. Line
intensities, partition sums, pressure and Doppler broadening
enter a Voigt absorption calculation with physical number-
density units. The source surface concentration is mapped to
an exponential gas proﬁle with 1.5 km scale height. Water
uses a 2 km proﬁle; PM uses 1 km and the previously declared
exploratory optical parameters. These proﬁles are assumptions.
Reference temperature, pressure and dew point are taken
from the training-only protocol. The explicit equations, line
exclusions and prior implementation checks appear in the
appendix.
The reference link for the full-grid comparison uses 256
conceptual frequencies from 60 to 400 GHz, 550 km altitude,
45◦elevation, 23 dBm total transmitted power, 0.5/0.3 m
transmit/receive apertures, 1 MHz per probe, 6 dB noise ﬁgure
and 5 dB implementation loss. The full-grid estimator retains
202 probes with reference SNR at least 5 dB; the sequential
comparison below uses 71 of these in its single band. Their
power is not redistributed after masking. Thus the current
study does not silently improve the link when opaque probes
are removed. The frequency grid is a multiband modeling
device, not a demonstrated contiguous waveform.

VIII. THREE NO2 INTERVENTIONS WITH CHARGED
ACQUISITION
The I2R proposal motivates layered HITRAN modeling,
statistical ratiometric inversion and sensitivity analysis; it
supplies no repeated receiver calibration records. We therefore


distinguish physical inputs, mathematical error models and
missing calibration evidence. A laboratory THz study illus-
trates why reference repeatability and frequency tuning require
separate characterization [18]; its methyl-chloride apparatus
does not calibrate our NO2 receiver.
A. Differencing and structured calibration
Temporal subtraction gives
y1 −y0 = D(θ1 −θ0) + N(β1 −β0) + (b1 −b0) + (n1 −n0).
Identical persistent instrumental error cancels. Independent
per-reading error boxes of radius ϵ instead leave a box of
radius 2ϵ. Concentration changes are the estimand; absolute
concentration additionally needs a baseline estimate and its co-
variance. Independent readings with equal noise have doubled
variance at equal per-reading dwell. At equal total acquisition
time, halving the budget of each reading gives four times
the variance before extra retuning. We use 2 conv{±bs} for
atmospheric difference error, so the design does not assume
atmospheric cancellation.
Spectral differencing alone is a coordinate change on the
offset-free observation space. Propagating the full contrast co-
variance gives a NO2 GLS variance ratio of 1.000000000005
relative to the original observations with an unknown offset.
Treating shared-reference contrasts as independent would cre-
ate a spurious gain.
For structured calibration write bcal = Sγ + u, |γk| ≤1
and ∥u∥∞≤ϵr. The exact support contribution is ∥ST h∥1 +
ϵr∥h∥1. Our exploratory S contains degree-zero, one and two
Legendre modes on 260–400 GHz, each scaled by (ϵ −ϵr)/3.
This set is a subset of the original radius-ϵ box. It rep-
resents stronger assumptions, not measured improvement in
an instrument. The constant mode is already canceled by
N. We retain ϵ = 10−4 dB with residual zero or 10−5
dB, and implement a separate chronological reference-sweep
characterization tool. No receiver recordings are available to
run that characterization.

IX. CONCLUSION
Joint dwell and frequency design improves the declared
ﬁnite-resource objective, but the ﬁne-grid NO2 bias ﬂoor
remains above one under 10−4 dB arbitrary persistent spectral
error. Structured calibration assumptions yield a constructive
conditional route at longer acquisition time; the required
residual bound is unmeasured. Temporal differences cancel
common bias and estimate concentration changes at a sub-
stantial noise cost. Real-weather stress further exposes failure
of the nominal SNR and atmospheric envelopes. CO and SO2
retain the earlier conditional margins, while NO2 remains a
feasibility and calibration-requirements study. Measured drift,
geometry control and synchronized attenuation truth are nec-
essary before claiming retrieval performance.

three tangent spectra of the complete modeled mean, including
the background. The reference uses only the training me-

offsets ±500 Pa and gas-height offsets ±100 m around 1.5 km.

mismatch grid. Applying the same whitened SVD estimator

C. Resource accounting and validation boundary
For a declared pilot duration Ts, the idealized simultaneous-
probe observation duration is NpTs and total transmitted
energy is PtotalNpTs. At the illustrative Ts = 1 µs and
23 dBm, 300 pilots require 0.3 ms and 0.0599 mJ; 30,000

tion, guards and frequency-window access are excluded. The
broad grid does not demonstrate that these probes can be
acquired simultaneously with one front end. The larger budgets
are therefore explicit resource scenarios, not free information
gains.

concentration/column truth, independent calibration recordings

and time, measured atmospheric proﬁles or justiﬁed uncer-

evaluation period. A future acceptance criterion must specify
the concentration averaging period and required estimation
error before that evaluation. These are missing inputs, not
completed experiments.

APPENDIX B
SUPPORTING PHYSICS AND EARLIER EXPERIMENTS
The following diagnostics document earlier questions and
parameter conventions. Their full-period reference atmosphere,
256-probe low-SNR extrapolation and historical comparisons
do not replace the training-only, 202-probe primary protocol
above. Old results are retained to disclose their limitations and
the evidence motivating the current question.
A. Related Work and Scope
Aliaga et al. integrated differential absorption radar with
beyond 100 GHz satellite communications and evaluated com-
munication and water vapour sensing tradeoffs using atmo-
spheric models [1]. Their work supports the shared sensing and
communication premise, but it neither uses the UCI pollutants
nor demonstrates their retrieval. Yang et al. model molecular
and particulate attenuation across space air ground media and
compare gaseous attenuation with ITU recommendations [2].
Their decomposition motivates our layered gas and particle
treatment, while also highlighting that particle composition
and size cannot be replaced by an arbitrary spectral trend.
ITU-R P.676-13 describes gaseous attenuation and ITU-
R P.835-7 supplies reference atmospheres [3], [4]. We use
them as propagation context, not as a claim that our trace
gas columns or exploratory PM parameters are ITU calibrated.
HITRAN2024 was the current edition when the local line table
was retrieved through HAPI in July 2026 [5]. HAPI supplies
partition sums and a reference implementation check using the
same spectroscopic records [6].
Dong et al. derive CRB and Bayesian CRB limits for Ku
band rain sensing from satellite pilot attenuation [7]. Their
pilot power variance model is methodologically relevant, but
their rain coefﬁcients and validation data are not transferred
to sub THz pollutants. The 0.63 dB residual value used below
is borrowed only as a scenario value. It is not calibrated for
the present frequency range or hardware. The gap addressed
here is thus an estimation limit study for multiple ambient
pollutants using public surface records and line data, with
simulated rather than measured link attenuation.

F. Results
1) Signal Magnitude: Fig. 4 shows excess attenuation at the
UCI ninety ﬁfth percentile concentration and 45◦elevation.
The strongest gas peak is only 0.03186 dB for CO. O3,
SO2, and NO2 peak at 0.00484, 0.01517, and 0.00113 dB.
Exploratory PM peaks are approximately 1.2×10−4 dB. Every
value is far below the assumed 0.63 dB residual component,
while total pilot error is larger still because of the ﬁrst term
in (24).
The most informative individual probes agree with the line
structure: CO information is concentrated near 345.33 GHz,
O3 near 358.67 GHz, SO2 near 357.33 GHz, and NO2 near
396 GHz, with additional NO2 information in lower loss
windows. Strong cross section alone is not sufﬁcient because
H2O/O2 background can also suppress SNR.
2) Reference Detection Floors: Table XII gives the main
result. All reference one sigma bounds exceed the correspond-
ing WHO concentration scales. The closest gas is CO at 20.8
times; NO2 is 2,577 times. Three sigma ratios are exactly
threefold larger. The PM values are already optimistic one
mode bounds; joint ﬁne and coarse retrieval is practically
nonidentiﬁable.
The comparison is intentionally only a scale test. WHO
values are health guidelines averaged over 8 h for O3 and
24 h for the other targets, while the CRB describes one
simulated attenuation burst and an assumed vertical mapping.
The ratios do not establish a regulatory limit of detection,
temporal averaging performance, or compliance classiﬁcation.

3) Sensitivity and Optimistic Stress Test: The elevation
sweep has a sensing optimum at the lowest tested elevation.
At 15◦, the longer assumed pollutant path outweighs the lower
link SNR, reducing one sigma gas ratios to 10.4 for CO,
135 for O3, 71.9 for SO2, and 1,294 for NO2. The result
is conditional on the plane parallel proﬁle; it is not a general
claim that the lowest elevation is operationally optimal.
Increasing pilots from 30 to 3,000 at 45◦reduces the CO
ratio from 20.8 to 12.8, after which the 0.63 dB residual
dominates. Increasing total power from 23 to 33 dBm changes
the CO ratio only to 20.4. Setting the residual to zero while
retaining 30 pilots gives 16.4. These one factor results show
that the gap is not simply a low SNR problem: weak and
overlapping sensitivities, opaque frequencies, pilot power es-
timation noise, and nuisance projection all matter.
We also combine every optimistic setting evaluated: 15◦,
3,000 pilots, zero residual, 33 dBm total power, and all 256
probes. This is a stress test, not a proposed deployment. Its one
sigma ﬂoor to guideline ratios are 0.649 for CO, 9.19 for O3,
4.35 for SO2, 88.2 for NO2, 3,538 for PM2.5, and 1,232 for
PM10. Only CO crosses the guideline concentration scale at
one sigma, and its three sigma ratio is 1.95. The combined case
therefore does not provide a robust multi pollutant detection
success.
4) Optimized Probes and H2O Control: Table XIII applies
greedy D optimal selection to the denser 1,024 point candidate
grid. At 256 probes, the reference gas ratios improve by factors
of 1.52 to 1.81 over the matched uniform selection, yet none
approaches one. In the combined optimistic case, optimized
CO reaches 0.353 at one sigma but remains 1.059 at three
sigma. The other three gases remain above their guideline
scales even at one sigma.
Numerical power optimization under the stated variance
model on the 32 selected probes adds only 0.2% to 4.3%
improvement over equal power D optimal selection. Changing
the regularizer from 10−6 to 10−12 leaves the 256 selected
frequencies and ﬂoors unchanged. For optimistic CO, a one
sigma ratio of one would require the independent residual to be
at most 0.223 dB; a three sigma ratio of one is unattainable
in this scenario even at zero residual. Reference case gases
remain above one even at zero residual.

6.36 × 109 and 1.45 × 109 µg m−3 for PM2.5 and PM10. The
optimistic one parameter PM bounds must not be read as joint

constrained estimator could remove negative outputs, but it
cannot create absent Fisher information.

8) Training-atmosphere correlated-noise sensitivity: Inde-
pendence is not a universal optimistic assumption when
marginal noise variances are ﬁxed. A correlated component

more damaging. For example, with two probes, target column
(1, −1)T , an unknown common offset, unit marginal variance
and a common residual variance 0.4, the target variance bound
is 0.3, compared with 0.5 for independent noise. A separate
numerical test veriﬁes this elementary counterexample.
